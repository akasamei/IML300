Woah animal protection law

Animal protection laws can meet WOAH (World Organisation for Animal Health) standards by integrating woah's science-based principles into their own legislation, focusing on the five domains of animal welfare, and implementing woah's recommendations for humane practices in areas like transport, slaughter, and production systems. This requires aligning national laws with international standards for disease control, animal health, and welfare by ensuring legislation supports key elements like surveillance, reporting, and the financing of animal health measures.

Five domains model:

  • Nutrition: evaluates access to proper food and water, and the animal's nutritional state.
  • Physical environment: assesses the animal's surroundings, including shelter, housing, and factors that can cause stress or discomfort.
  • Health: considers the animal's physical health, including disease prevention, injury, and pain.
  • Behavioral interactions: examines the opportunities for the animal to express natural behaviors, such as social interactions, play, and other species-specific behaviors.
  • Mental state: reflects the animal's emotional experience and internal feeling states, such as pleasure, fear, anxiety, or frustration.
