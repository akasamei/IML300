Vibe Coding Experiments

This page collects experimental websites made with AI-assisted tools and interactive platforms. Each project plays with ideas from class readings, critical code, and digital art practice.

1. Cat Feeding Website

I created an interactive “cat feeding” website. On the site, a cat is shown with a satisfaction percentage. The visitor’s goal is to feed the cat until it’s fully satisfied, and also remember to clean up after the cat when it poops.

Cat Feeding Website

2. Aspirin, Laptops, and Digital Art

Inspired by the reading “What Screens Want,” this project uses the evolution of aspirin as a metaphor for the iteration of laptops and digital art. On the website, you can “feed” the computer with aspirin to update it, and underneath, the device information is provided along with a piece of digital art from that time period.

Aspirin & Screen Evolution

3. Computer History Timeline

I used Lovable to create an interactive computer-history timeline website with a modern, tech-inspired design. It features clickable timeline nodes that reveal detailed information about each era of computing. Overall, it understood my command better than Claude, but it took a long time to actually generate the website.

Computer History Timeline

4. Pixel Surveillance & Black Women

This Lovable website randomly generates small pixelated characters, and users have to click on the Black women characters to see what they are doing (surveillance). It was inspired by the reading by Sydette Harry, which discusses the surveillance and observation of Black women in particular.

Surveillance Pixel World

5. Comment Storm & Unerasable Characters

This website is inspired by Winnie Soon’s Unerasable Characters. She explores government propaganda and erasure around Covid-19. Data and news were deleted by the government in order to “protect” people. Covid originally started at the end of December 2019, but China did not officially report it until February, when thousands and millions of people had already died. In response, I made a website that generates random letters, but when the audience clicks “delete,” it actually adds more comments. In reality, data and news can never be fully hidden; they eventually surface.

Comment Storm Visualizer

6. Many Eyes: Algorithmic Personas

This project is inspired by Joy Buolamwini, who discovered algorithmic bias embedded in AI systems. This creative website allows people to upload an image of themselves, and different AI “personas” generate descriptions based on the image to reveal their algorithmic personalities and biases.

Many Eyes Art

7. Single Story Satire

This website is inspired by Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie’s TED Talk “The Danger of a Single Story.” She explains how hearing only one repeated narrative about a person, place, or culture creates stereotypes and distorts reality. The website asks for audiences’ ethnicity, citizenship, cultural background, and gender, then creates a stereotypical story based on their choices. At the end, it asks the audience to rewrite their own story, because people shouldn’t be reduced to backgrounds and stereotypes.

Single Story Satire

8. Moonlight Sonata Canvas

This website is inspired by Christine Sun Kim. After watching her TED Talk, I connected her story with Beethoven—a pianist who became deaf but continued to compose masterpieces like Moonlight Sonata. The website includes a visualizer of the piece and more information about the composition and Beethoven.

Moonlight Sonata Canvas

9. Satellite Space Diaries

Inspired by Xin Liu’s art of creating diaries for satellites, this work explores more satellites’ space journeys and personifies them through personal diaries. It also gives audiences who are not in space-related fields a chance to learn about human space navigation.

Space Diary Friends

10. Groove Draw Flow

For “vibe coding,” I was inspired by Olivia Jack’s patterned work. I originally wanted it to be an aesthetically pleasing website where people could draw a pattern and have it repeat and move, forming an interesting visual. But I’m guessing the AI could only understand the interactive drawing part, and not fully design the visual pattern the way a human would.

Groove Draw Flow
