Project 1: Interactive Visualizer (p5.js)

For project 1, I designed a beat visualizer for my favorite K-pop girl band: Blackpink. I used Blackpink’s concert picture as the background of the visualizer and chose 3 of their songs to play as the background music. When the music starts, pink circular shapes will generate based on the music’s beat, and when the audience clicks on a random space on the screen, it will generate a random artist’s name from Blackpink. Moreover, when the audience clicks directly on the generated circular shape, confetti will also fall. This whole piece aims to mimic an online concert, where the audience can interact with the music as if they are in a real-life Blackpink concert.

Project 2: HyperNarrative

This project is an interactive story about the life of a kitten. The story begins when a kitten is born and must choose where to live — on the street, in an apartment, or in a big house. Each choice leads to a different storyline and ending, showing how the kitten’s fate changes depending on human actions, whether it is cared for, ignored, or harmed. In the story, each path represents a different type of human environment: the street reflects human neglect and urban indifference, the apartment shows unstable affection and cruelty, and the big house represents material comfort without emotional responsibility. These situations mirror real social issues such as pet abandonment, animal abuse, and the way people treat animals as objects instead of lives with feelings.

Project 3: Networked Care

This website explores how animal cruelty is embedded in everyday human spaces—oceans, labs, farms, wild habitats, entertainment, and even our homes. Instead of hiding violence, it brings it to the surface, asking us to confront how normalized and invisible it often becomes. Animals here are not background images but beings whose bodies are reshaped by human desires for profit, pleasure, food, fashion, and convenience. The project poses a central question: what happens if we stop looking away and try to see daily systems from animals’ perspectives rather than our own comfort? This is not a full catalog of animal suffering or a guide with easy solutions. Instead, it creates a space of discomfort and reflection, inviting viewers to recognize their own position in networks of harm and consider change. A trigger warning notes that some pages include disturbing text, imagery, and video, and encourages visitors to care for their emotional well-being.
